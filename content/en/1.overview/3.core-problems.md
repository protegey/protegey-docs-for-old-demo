# Core Problems Solved

Protegey focuses on specific, high-impact fraud typologies that thrive in the gaps between institutions.

## 1. Synthetic Identity Fraud

**The Challenge**: Fraudsters create identities using a mix of real and fake data (e.g., a real SSN with a fake name). These identities often pass standard credit bureau checks because they have no negative history.

**The Protegey Solution**: By tracking the usage of identity elements across the network, Protegey can detect when a single SSN is associated with multiple names or when a "new" identity shows impossible velocity across different institutions.

## 2. Account Takeover (ATO)

**The Challenge**: Using stolen credentials, attackers gain access to legitimate accounts. Because the login looks "correct" (correct password, sometimes even intercepted 2FA), it is hard to detect in isolation.

**The Protegey Solution**: If a device or IP address is flagged for ATO activity at Partner A, Protegey immediately warns Partner B if that same device attempts to log in, even if the credentials are correct.

## 3. First-Party Fraud (Busting Out)

**The Challenge**: A legitimate customer intentionally builds credit and then maxes out all lines with no intention of paying.

**The Protegey Solution**: Protegey detects the "velocity of application" and credit usage patterns that precede a bust-out, notifying lenders of the elevated risk before the final run-up occurs.

## 4. Application Fraud

**The Challenge**: Attackers use botnets to apply for thousands of accounts using stolen or generated identities.

**The Protegey Solution**: Network-wide velocity rules detect the coordinated nature of the attack, identifying the common thread (e.g., device fingerprint, IP subnet, email pattern) that links the applications.

## 5. Mule Account Networks

**The Challenge**: Accounts are opened solely to launder money from other fraud schemes.

**The Protegey Solution**: Protegey traces the flow of risk. When funds move from a known high-risk entity to a new account, that new account inherits a portion of the risk score, flagging it for review as a potential mule.
